## 📌 What is a Computer Worm?
A **worm** is a standalone type of malware that self-replicates and spreads across computer networks entirely on its own. Unlike a virus, a worm **does not require human interaction** to run or propagate, and it does not need to attach itself to an existing host program.

---

## ⚙️ How a Worm Operates
Worms take advantage of automation and network vulnerabilities to move fast:

1. **Scanning:** The worm scans the local network or the internet looking for devices running unpatched vulnerabilities (like an open port or outdated service).
2. **Exploitation:** It uses an exploit to force its way into the vulnerable device.
3. **Payload Delivery:** It drops its malicious code onto the new machine.
4. **Execution & Loop:** The code runs instantly, turns that new machine into a launching pad, and immediately begins scanning for the next target.

---

## 💥 Impact: Network Exhaustion
While worms can carry payloads that delete data, their primary damage often comes from sheer traffic volume. Because they replicate so quickly, they consume massive amounts of network bandwidth and memory, causing entire corporate networks, routers, and servers to crash under the load.

> Understanding the language used by cybersecurity professionals.

## 1. Threat

A potential cause of harm to a system, network, or data.

**Example:** An attacker attempting to steal sensitive information.

## 2. Vulnerability

A weakness in a system that could be exploited.

**Example:** An outdated application with a known security flaw.

## 3. Exploit

A technique or method used to take advantage of a vulnerability.

**Example:** Using a known software flaw to gain unauthorized access.

## 4. Risk

The potential for loss or damage when a threat exploits a vulnerability.

**Example:** Losing confidential information because of an insecure database.

## 5. Attack Surface

All the points where an attacker could potentially attempt to access or compromise a system.

**Example:** Open ports, web applications, and exposed services.

## 6. Malware

Software designed to perform malicious activities.

Common types:

- Virus
- Worm
- Trojan
- Ransomware
- Spyware

## 7. Phishing

A social engineering technique that tricks people into revealing sensitive information.

**Example:** A fake email asking someone to verify their bank password.

## 8. Authentication

The process of verifying the identity of a user or system.

**Example:** Logging in using a username and password.

## 9. Authorization

The process of determining what an authenticated user is allowed to access or do.

**Example:** A normal employee cannot access administrator settings.

## 10. Encryption

The process of converting readable information (plaintext) into an unreadable form (ciphertext) using a cryptographic method.

**Example:** Encrypting confidential files.
